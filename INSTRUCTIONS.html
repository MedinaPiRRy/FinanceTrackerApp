FinanceTracker: how to use it

FinanceTracker keeps track of your money on your own computer. You bring in your bank's transactions, the app sorts them, and you see where your money goes, what you owe and where you are heading. Nothing is sent over the internet, and there is no account to create. Everything you do here can be undone or fixed; the few things that cannot are clearly marked.

Short on time? Read Your first 10 minutes, then come back when you need something.

Your first 10 minutes

  1. Open the app. On the Welcome screen click Explore with sample data to look around with made-up people and transactions, or click Use my own data to start for real. Sample data is kept separate and one click removes it (see Sample data or your own data).
  2. For your own data, choose Just me, Me and my partner, or Me, my partner and a shared household, type your name(s), and click Continue to import.
  3. Download a statement from your bank as a CSV or Excel file (see Download a file from your bank).
  4. Click Import in the left menu, choose the account, choose the file and check the preview. Then click Import at the bottom of the preview.
  5. Click Review in the left menu and answer the few questions the app has about money it could not understand (see Review).
  6. Click Dashboard to see your month. You will see income, spending and where the money went.
  7. Click Budget, then Suggest budgets from my last 3 months for a quick start (see Budgets).
  8. Click Settings, then General, and turn on Automatic backup if it is not on already (see Backups and restore).

Find your problem

Click the line that sounds like your problem.

  • My balance does not match my bank
  • A transaction is in the wrong category
  • I imported the same file twice
  • Some transactions are missing from my totals
  • A payment to my loan did not lower the loan
  • Interest or tax is counted wrongly
  • I deleted or changed something by mistake
  • How do I back up, or move to a new computer?
  • How do I start over?

Getting started

Welcome screen

When you open the app for the first time you see Welcome to FinanceTracker with two choices:

  • Explore with sample data: fills the app with made-up people and about six months of transactions so you can look around. Nothing you do there touches real data.
  • Use my own data: you set up your own people and accounts, then bring in statements from your bank.

[screenshot: Welcome screen]

Sample data or your own data

Sample data lives in its own place and never mixes with your own. While you are in sample data, a bar at the top says You are exploring sample data. To leave it, click Start with my own data in that bar, or go to Settings, Backup & data and click the same-named button Start with my own data. You can try the sample again later by starting fresh.

Who is this for?

After Use my own data you pick one of three setups:

  • Just me: one person tracking their own money.
  • Me and my partner: two people, each with their own private finances, side by side. Money you send each other is treated as movement between you, not as income or spending.
  • Me, my partner and a shared household: the same, plus shared accounts (a joint account or card), a household budget and goals you share. See Household view.

You can change your mind later under Settings, People & setup.

Names and accounts

  1. Type Your name (and Your partner's name if you chose a setup with a partner). The screen is called Your name and accounts for just me and Your names and accounts with a partner.
  2. Under Accounts you will see a short list (for example Chequing and Credit card). Change the name or type of any of them, click Remove next to ones you do not have, or click Add an account for more. Types are Chequing, Savings, Credit card, Investment and Other.
  3. Click Continue to import. You land on the Import page, where you can bring in your first statement.

A Cash wallet is created for you automatically. You can add, rename or close accounts at any time, so do not worry about getting this perfect (see Accounts).

Changing a name later

Click Settings, then General. Under Names, change a name and click Save name. Changing a name never changes your data.

Finding your way around

  • The left menu lists every page. Click a name to open it. A small number next to Review or Recurring means something is waiting for you.
  • ← Back and Forward → (top of the window) work like a browser: Back returns to the page you were on, with the same filters, month and page number. Keyboard: Alt + Left arrow and Alt + Right arrow.
  • Light / Dark (top right) switches between the Light and Dark looks. If you picked one of the other styles, the button there says Style and takes you to the picker. See Appearance for all eight styles.
  • Click almost any figure. Numbers on the Dashboard, Monthly review and other pages open the transactions behind them.
  • Long lists show 50 rows at a time. Use Previous, Next, « and » under the list. The app remembers your page when you press Back.
  • Who you are looking at. With a partner, the buttons at the top (your name, your partner's name and Household) switch whose finances you see.

Importing statements

Importing means bringing the transactions from your bank into the app. You do it every month or so. Nothing is saved until you click the final button, so you can look first.

Download a file from your bank

  1. Sign in to your bank's website or app.
  2. Open the account (chequing, savings or credit card) and find Download, Export or Transactions.
  3. Choose the dates you want and the format CSV (best) or Excel. Pick Spreadsheet or CSV, not PDF: the app cannot read PDF statements.
  4. Save the file somewhere you can find it, such as your Downloads folder.

Import the file

  1. Click Import in the left menu.
  2. Under Account this statement belongs to, choose the account. You will see Choose account… until you do. If you pick the file first, the app may choose for you and say, for example, "The file name matches Chequing. Change it above if that is wrong."
  3. Under Statement file (.csv, .xlsx), click and choose your file. The preview appears.
  4. If your spending shows as plus numbers in the file (some banks do this), tick Spending shows as positive numbers in this file. Leave it off unless the preview shows your purchases as money coming in.
  5. Look over the preview (next section), then click Import N transactions at the bottom.
  6. You see Imported N transactions and how many need your review, with a link Open review queue.

[screenshot: Import preview]

Read the preview

The boxes at the top say how many rows are in the file, how many Will be imported, how many are Already in the app (left out so you do not get doubles) and how many Need attention.

Below that, click All, New, Already in app or Need attention to filter. Each row shows:

  • Import: untick to leave a row out.
  • Name: the cleaned-up name. You can type a better one.
  • Type: Expense, Income, Refund / reimbursement, Transfer (money moved between your own accounts), or Decide later.
  • Category: suggested from what the app has learned about that shop. Change it if you like. Click New category and Add if you need one that does not exist.
  • Status: Ready, Needs a category or Needs review. Rows that need attention are still saved; they simply wait in Review.
What does "Need attention" mean?

The app is not sure what the row is. For example, an e-transfer (a payment sent by email) could be rent, a gift, or money to your partner. The app never guesses these. They wait in Review and are not counted as income or spending until you decide.

Duplicates

The app never records the same transaction twice. If you import a file again, or an overlapping one, rows that are already there show as Already in the app and are left out. If you choose a file you imported before, a note at the top says This exact file was already imported (with the date and account) and that importing it again adds nothing twice. If you chose a different account than last time, the note warns that every row would look new and asks you to tick Import it here anyway before you can continue. If every row is already there, there is nothing left to import: click Cancel. Two real purchases that look identical on the same day (two coffees at the same shop) are both kept. If you are unsure, look at the Past imports list under the preview.

Undo an import

Under Past imports, find the file and click Undo import. The transactions of that import are removed; everything else stays.

Example. You downloaded your September chequing CSV and imported it. On the 5th of October you download a new file that starts on 25 September. Import it the same way: the overlapping days show as Already in the app, and only the new ones are added.

Review

Review is where the app asks you about money it could not understand. Until you decide, those transactions are not counted as income or spending. A number next to Review in the left menu tells you how many are waiting. Click Review to open the queue.

The tabs

  • Possible income: money that came in and the app cannot tell what it was (e-transfers received, deposits). Is it income, a reimbursement, or something else?
  • Money in: other money coming in that needs a category.
  • Money out: money that went out and needs a category or a decision (e-transfers sent, withdrawals).
  • Transfers & investments: money moved to or from an investment or another account.
  • Older: things that have waited a long time (see below).

Each tab groups transactions by source (the person or shop), with a count and a total, so one decision can clear many.

Decide one transaction

  1. Click a tab, find a source and look at the question (for example "Deposit: where did this money come from?").
  2. Under This is…, choose what it is. For money in: Income (earned or received), Reimbursement or refund (reduces spending), Transfer between my own or shared accounts (not income), or Not income: just take it off this list (it stays out of your income and spending). For money out: Expense, Transfer between my own or shared accounts / investment, or It is fine as it is: just take it off this list. The two last choices change nothing about the transaction; they only clear it from the Review list. If you have a partner there is also Between us (money to or from your partner). That money counts as movement between you, not as income or spending.
  3. If it asks for a Category, choose one, or type a new one and click Add.
  4. Optional: type a Note.
  5. Click Save. The transaction leaves the queue and now counts.

Use See it in the account if you want to look at the line in your list of transactions first.

Always do this

When you decide, you will see a box: Always do this for (the source's name). Tick it and click Save. Everything else waiting from that source, and every future import, is filed the same way, in the same direction (money in or money out). Example: tick it for your pay deposit once, and every future pay cheque is filed as income automatically.

To undo it, click Settings, then Remembered sources, and click Forget next to the source. Nothing already filed changes; only the future.

Loans in Review

If a payment is for a loan you track (for example your student loan), choose Expense, then under Counts toward a loan I track (optional) choose the loan. The payment stays an expense in its category and lowers the loan. See Loans and personal debts.

Older transactions

Transactions that have waited longer than a number of days (90 unless you change it) are set aside: they are not highlighted and not counted. They sit under the Older tab, and any page that shows a month with such transactions shows a note: "N transactions from that month are still waiting for review (older than 90 days), so they are not counted. This month's figures may not match your bank statements," with a Review them button. Review them whenever you like. To change the number of days, click Settings, General, and change Waiting transactions count as "older" after.

Transactions

Transactions is your full list of money in and out. Click Transactions in the left menu.

Search and filter

Above the list you can use Search (what the description says), Account, Category, Type, From, To, Min $, Max $, and Needs review only. Click Clear to remove all filters. Click a column heading (Date, Description, Category, Amount) to sort. The list shows 50 at a time; use the page buttons underneath. The line "N transactions · net …" shows how many match and their total.

Change one category

Click the category in the row and choose another. The change is saved at once, and the app remembers it for the next import of that shop.

Change many at once

  1. Tick the boxes at the left of the rows (the box in the heading ticks the whole page). To pick everything that matches your filters, tick one row and click Select all N that match.
  2. A bar appears. With Move selected, choose Move to category, or under Or create one type a new name, choose whether it is spending or income (and whether it goes under a main category) and click Add.
  3. Optional: tick Also remember these names for future imports.
  4. Click Move N. You see "Moved N transactions". Nothing is deleted, the transactions just move.

Edit or delete many

In the same bar, click Edit to give selected transactions a New merchant name, change their Type (Spending, Income, Refund, Transfer between my accounts) or Category, then Save changes to N. Click Taxes & interest to count them as taxes or interest (see Taxes and interest). Click Delete to remove them; you are asked to confirm and told how many. Deleting cannot be undone (but an import can be undone from the Import page, which removes a whole file at once).

Jump in from other pages

Almost every figure in the app is clickable and lands here with the right filters already set: a category on the Dashboard, a budget, a goal, an insight, a recurring bill. In Settings, Categories, the Transactions button next to a category opens everything filed in it. Use ← Back to return.

Categories and subcategories

A category is a label such as Groceries or Rent & housing. A subcategory sits under one, for example Gas under Transportation. Subcategories count toward their main category in budgets, charts and the forecast, but you can still look at them on their own.

  1. Click Settings, then Categories.
  2. Click Spending or Income to choose which list.
  3. To add one, type a name under New spending category (or New income category), optionally choose a main category in Under, and click Add.
  4. For each category you can choose a Budget, say what it Counts as (see Taxes and interest), or click Transactions, Rename, Move (under another category, or back to a main one) or Delete.

Renaming a category renames it everywhere. Deleting a category that has transactions asks you which category to move them to first, so nothing is lost.

Example. You want to see what you spend on perfume separately from the rest of Health & personal care. Add a category called Perfumes with Under set to Health & personal care. Then move your perfume purchases into it (see Change many at once). Health & personal care keeps counting them in its total.

Rules by amount

Sometimes the amount tells you what was bought. At a gas station, $12 is probably a snack and $70 is gas. A rule by amount files the purchase for you.

  1. Click Settings, then Rules by amount, then Add a rule.
  2. Type a Name (for example "Gas stations").
  3. Under Words in the merchant's name type the words, separated by commas, for example PETRO, SHELL, ESSO.
  4. Under Amount ($) type the dividing amount, for example 30.
  5. Choose That much or less is (for example Snacks) and More than that is (for example Gas).
  6. Click Save.

New imports follow the rule. A purchase you have categorised yourself is never changed. To apply it to what is already in the app, click Apply… next to the rule: you see how many purchases it would change, and nothing changes until you click Apply. A tick-box lets you also re-file purchases the app had categorised on its own.

Cash and tips

Use this for money that never touches a bank: tips, cash you spend, bills you pay in cash. Click Cash & tips. At the top, Cash on hand shows tips recorded minus cash spent or deposited.

  • Spent cash: fill in Date, Amount ($), What for, Category and click Record spending. It counts as spending in that category and comes out of your cash.
  • Add tips for a day: choose the Date, the Currency (CAD (home) or another one), the Amount ($) and click Save tips. For another currency you type the rate you got, and the Canadian-dollar value is what counts; the original amount stays visible.
  • Deposit cash to the bank: choose the date, amount and the account under Into, then click Deposit. This moves the money; it is not new income, so deposited tips are never counted twice.
  • Recent cash activity lists everything. Click Delete on an entry you typed by mistake.

Recurring payments

Recurring means payments that come back on a schedule: rent, subscriptions, your phone plan, your pay. Click Recurring.

  • Found N regular payments: the app spots repeating payments in your transactions. Tick the ones you want to track and click Track N selected. Click Not recurring on one it should never suggest again.
  • Add item: add one yourself. Fill in Name, Type (Bill or subscription or Income), Amount ($), How often, Paid from, optionally a Note, and click Add item.
  • Did these stop? If a tracked payment stops appearing, the app asks. Click Yes, it was cancelled or No, it is still active.
  • Refunded charges: charges your bank pays back every time (a monthly fee and its rebate). You can Track anyway or Hide.
  • Bills and subscriptions lists what you track with the cost per month and per year. Use Edit and Delete next to an item.

The total at the bottom of a list is your monthly and yearly cost. The Dashboard and the Forecast use it.

Budgets

A budget is the most you plan to spend on one or more categories in a month.

  1. Click Budget, then Add budget.
  2. Type a Name and the Monthly amount ($).
  3. Tick the Spending categories covered. A category can be in only one budget.
  4. Click Save budget. You see it in the list with a bar showing how much is used. The bar goes amber when you are near the limit and red when you are over.

Use Month (top right) to look at other months. Click a budget's name to see its transactions, split by account. Use Edit or Delete on a budget (your transactions are not affected). Spending with no budget lists categories you spent in that are not in any budget; click View to see them.

Suggested budgets

Click Suggest budgets from my last 3 months. The app shows each category's monthly average, rounded up to the next $5. Untick what you do not want, change any amount, and click Create N budgets. Nothing is saved until you click that.

Goals

A goal is something you are working toward. Click Goals, then Add goal.

  1. Type a Name.
  2. Choose the Type:
    • Savings I add to myself: you type how much you have saved so far.
    • Track an account: the goal follows an account's balance (for example a savings account).
    • Count spending in a category: counts spending in a category (for example wedding payments).
    • Pay off cards and loans: tick the cards and loans to pay off; progress starts from what is owed today.
  3. Type the Target ($), and optionally a Deadline and a Planned per month ($, optional).
  4. Click Save goal.

Each goal shows how far along it is, what is still needed per month to meet the deadline, and when you would finish at your planned amount. Click a goal to see the transactions that count toward it, how many and how much. Edit and Delete are on each goal (your accounts and transactions are not affected).

Goal ideas

Click Get goal ideas. The app suggests realistic goals worked out from your own numbers (paying down a loan, an emergency fund, growing an investment) with a monthly amount that never uses more than 60% of what you have left over each month. Change any Per month ($), then click Add this goal on the ones you want.

Debt payoff planner

At the bottom of Goals, the planner shows how long it takes to clear your cards and loans. It starts from typical interest rates and minimum payments, and you can change them: type your real Interest rate and Minimum payment for each debt, and How much to pay toward debts each month. Choose an Order: Highest interest first (cheapest), Smallest balance first (quickest wins) or Minimums only. You see when you would be debt-free and the interest you would pay under each. Click Save plan to keep your numbers, or Use the suggested plan to go back. Show month-by-month schedule lists every month. Click a debt's name to see the payments made on it.

Dashboard

The first page you see. It answers "how is this month going?"

  • Month (top right) picks the month.
  • The boxes show Income, Spending, Net (income minus spending, with your savings rate), Accounts (what your accounts hold and what is owed on cards and loans), Net worth, Budget left and Recurring bills. Click any box to see what is behind it.
  • Compared with puts this month beside last month, the same month last year and your six-month average.
  • Charts show Income vs spending, Where the money went and Cumulative net. Click a bar or point to see its transactions. Click Table under a chart for the numbers.
  • Largest expenses, Biggest recurring bills and a few Insights are at the bottom.

A yellow bar saying "N transactions need your review" means some money is not counted yet: click Review now.

Monthly review

Click Monthly review for a one-page summary of a month: whether you came out ahead, Budget performance, What changed from last month, Biggest expenses, Biggest spending categories, Recurring bills (paid or not seen yet), Financial goals and Insights. Pick the month at the top right. Every name and figure is clickable.

Insights

Click Insights for patterns the app notices in your own numbers, such as "Dining spending fell 59% compared with September". Use All, Worth a look, Going well and Just facts to filter. Click one to open it: you see What this means in plain words, the transactions or budget behind it and a button to open the right page. Pick another month at the top right. A tip may also appear in a bar at the top when you open the app; click What does this mean? or Another tip, or close it with the x.

Accounts

An account is anywhere your money sits: chequing, savings, a credit card, a loan, cash, an investment. Click Accounts. At the top: Money in accounts, Owed on cards and loans and Personal debts.

Add an account

  1. Click Add account.
  2. Type a Name, choose the Type (Chequing, Savings, Credit card, Investment, Loan or Other), and optionally Bank or company.
  3. Type Balance now ($) (or Owed right now ($) for a card), and a Credit limit for a card if you want.
  4. Click Add account.

Balances come from the transactions you import, plus the starting balance. Investments are different: they move with the market, so you type their value yourself. On an investment's card, type Current value ($) and the date under As of, then click Save value; update it whenever you check.

Close, rename and share

On each account's card:

  • Rename: change its name.
  • Close account: removes it from your totals and lists but keeps its history. If it still holds or owes money you are asked to confirm first. Closed accounts appear under Closed accounts at the bottom of the page, with a Reopen button. To remove an account for good, use Delete in Settings, Accounts (you choose to move or delete its transactions).
  • Change limit (cards): set or change the credit limit. You then see "23% used" and how much is available.
  • View transactions: opens the transactions of that account.
  • Make shared: after you confirm, the account and all its transactions move to the household so they count for both of you (see Household view). A shared account shows Make Alex's and Make Sam's (your names) to give it back to one person.

To delete an account, or to see all of them in one table, click Settings, then Accounts. If an account has transactions, you are asked whether to move them to another account or delete them too, and you must type a word to confirm. A backup is made first.

Match my bank's balance

Reconciling means checking that the app's balance equals your bank's. If they differ, usually some transactions are missing.

  1. Open your bank and note today's balance. For a credit card, note how much you owe.
  2. In Accounts, click Match my bank's balance on the account's card.
  3. Type the amount in Balance your bank shows ($) (or Amount you owe, as your bank shows ($) for a card).
  4. If your bank lists pending charges (purchases not finished yet, which the bank leaves out of its balance), type them under Pending charges, if your bank lists them ($). This is optional. It is only for information: the app matches on what has posted, and tells you what you will owe when the pending ones post.
  5. Click Check. You see what the app shows, what your bank shows, and what the starting balance (what the account held before its first transaction) would have to be.
  6. If the change makes sense, click Set the starting balance. Only the starting balance changes; none of your transactions do. (The form says this too.)

If you see a warning (for example "This would make the card START with a credit … which is not possible"), do not set it. It means payments or other transactions are missing: import the missing statements first.

If your balance still doesn't match
  • Check that every statement from the first day to today has been imported for that account (see Past imports on the Import page).
  • Check that payments between your accounts are both there (a card payment leaves your chequing and arrives on the card).
  • Check Review for waiting transactions.
  • On Accounts, a box may say "The same transactions in two accounts": it lists identical rows held by two accounts (for example the same file imported into two cards). Click Delete on the copies from the account that does not own them. A backup is made first.

Loans and personal debts

Under Loans and personal debts you track money you owe that is not on a card: a student loan, a family loan.

  1. Click Add a loan or personal debt.
  2. Type Owed to, Amount owed ($) and an optional note, then click Add.
  3. Whenever the lender's balance changes, click Update balance next to the loan, type the new balance and date, and click Save. The history is kept.
  4. Click Details (or the loan's name) to see what it started at, what is owed now, how much is paid down (with a bar), every payment counted toward it, your average monthly payment, about how many months are left at that pace (interest is not counted), and a balance chart. History and Payments show the same things in short.

Example. You pay your student loan from your chequing account. To make that payment count: on Review, choose Expense, then under Counts toward a loan I track (optional) choose Student loan, tick Always do this if you want it every time, and click Save. The payment stays an expense in its category and the loan's balance goes down by that amount, which you can see under Details. To undo it, open Details and click Stop counting next to the payment.

What you own and net worth

Net worth is what you own minus what you owe. At the bottom of Accounts, Net worth and what you own shows your net worth, what is in your accounts, the value of houses, cars and investments you add, and what is owed.

  1. Click Add a house, car or investment.
  2. Type What is it, choose the Kind (House or property, Car or vehicle, Stocks or investments, Something else), Worth ($) and the date under As of, then click Add.
  3. Later, click Update value to record a new value; History shows a chart of past values. Rename and Remove are there too.

Removing an item does not affect your accounts or transactions. Net worth also appears on the Dashboard.

Taxes and interest

This page shows how much you paid and received in taxes and in interest, so you can see what they really cost you. Click Taxes & interest in the left menu. At the top, pick the Year.

  • Taxes in (year) and Interest in (year) show the net, with how much was paid and how much was received. Received counts money coming back to you as part of your taxes: a GST/HST credit, a government payment, a tax refund. It also counts interest you earn. Refunds reduce what you paid.
  • By month shows a chart and a table.
  • Taxes and Interest each list where the money went. Switch By category or By account.

See the payments behind a figure

Click the paid or received figure under a box, a category, an account name, or See every payment. A list opens under the page with each payment, why it counts (You marked it, Its category, or Its wording says interest), and its amount. Click a payment's name to see it in your transactions.

Count a payment as taxes or interest

  1. Click Add payments… (top right of the page).
  2. Type what the payment says under Search (for example "CRA", "GST credit" or "mortgage interest").
  3. Tick the payments.
  4. Click Count as taxes or Count as interest. Money you paid counts as paid; money you received counts as received. Back to automatic removes your choice.

You can also do this on Transactions: tick rows, click Taxes & interest, and choose Count as taxes, Count as interest, Do not count or Back to automatic. A small tag on the row shows when you have chosen.

To count a whole category, click Settings, Categories, and change Counts as to Taxes, Interest or Neither. This works for income categories too, which is how a "Government payments" category you receive counts as taxes received.

Leave something out

Everything counted can be taken out, and nothing is deleted: the transactions stay in your list. The page reminds you of this under the two figure boxes.

  • One payment: click Remove next to it.
  • Several: tick them and click Stop counting.
  • A whole category or account: click Stop counting next to its name, or Stop counting this whole category / Stop counting this whole account at the bottom of its list.

Everything you left out appears in a box called Left out. Click See them and Count again (or Count it again for an account) to put it back.

How does the app decide what counts?

In this order: your own choice on a payment wins. Then an account you took out is left out. Then the category's setting, or a guess from its name (Income tax, Property tax, HST and similar are taxes; a category named only Interest is interest). Finally, a payment you made whose own words say "interest" counts as interest, unless it says refund, reversal, rebate, credit, "no interest" or "interest-free". A category called "Bank fees & interest" is not counted as interest as a whole, because most of it is fees: only its lines that say interest count. A bank fee the bank gave straight back cancels out.

Example. You get a GST/HST credit every three months and want it in your taxes. Click Settings, Categories, Income, find the category that holds it, and change Counts as to Taxes (received). On Taxes & interest you now see it under received, and the net is what you really paid.

Forecast

Click Forecast to see where your money is heading from today's balances. Nothing here changes your accounts. Look ahead chooses 12, 24, 36 or 60 months. You see Spendable cash and Debt remaining charts, and a Compared table of three versions:

  • Today's pace: your recent income and spending continue, and only minimum payments are made on debts.
  • With my budgets & goals (My plan): follows your budgets, the monthly amounts you planned for goals and your debt payoff plan.
  • My what-if: your own version, below.

Month by month lists every month in the version you pick. How this is worked out explains the method in plain words. It is an estimate from your own history, not a promise.

What-ifs

  1. Click Try a what-if.
  2. Choose where to Start from (My budgets & goals or Today's pace).
  3. Move the sliders All income and Everyday spending (percent more or less), or type Extra toward debt each month ($).
  4. Untick an income or spending line to remove it, or type a new amount under Change to.
  5. Add something new (a second job, a new bill) under Add something new, or a one-off event (a car repair, a bonus) under One-off events.
  6. Watch the numbers change. Click Save what-if to keep it for next time or Clear to remove it.

Example. You are thinking of cancelling a $65.99 phone plan. Click Try a what-if, untick Phone plan in the spending lines, and compare the cash at the end.

Household view

If you chose Me and my partner, the buttons at the top of the window show your name, your partner's name and, with a shared household, Household. Each person has private finances. The Household view combines both:

  • Overview: Combined income, Combined spending, Net together, Money in accounts, Net worth, who brought in and spent what, and a chart of household spending by person.
  • Transactions, Import (shared) and Review (shared) for the shared accounts.
  • Accounts: every account, grouped by whose it is (Alex's accounts, Sam's accounts, Shared accounts).
  • Budget: household budgets cover category groups. Because you each have your own category names, a group is a shared name that joins them. See Category groups at the bottom of the page and type the group for each category.
  • Goals: goals you share, plus each person's goals (read-only here).

Money between you

Money you send each other is movement, not income or spending for either of you. When it shows up in Review, choose Between us. Click Settings, People & setup to type the names that show up on e-transfers between you, one per line; the app then recognises them by itself. It also learns a name the first time you choose Between us.

With a partner you can click Add the shared household. Settings

Click Settings in the left menu. You see a list of sections; click one to open it, and click ← Back to Settings to return.

General settings

Click General.

  • Names: change a person's name and click Save name.
  • Open the app on: the page you see first (Dashboard, Monthly review, Transactions, Review queue, Accounts, Budget or Forecast).
  • Waiting transactions count as "older" after: 30 days, 90 days (default), 6 months or 12 months (see Older transactions).
  • Automatic backup: Off, or Keep the last 5, 10 or 20. A backup is made the first time the app opens each day. It is on (last 10) unless you change it.

Appearance

Click Appearance. Each style is a small card showing its real colours. Click one (or Tab to it and use the arrow keys) and the whole app changes at once. The one in use is marked ✓ In use, and your choice is remembered on this computer.

  1. Click Settings, then Appearance.
  2. Click the style you like.
  3. To go back, click Match system or any other card.
  • Match system: Light or Dark, whichever your computer is set to.
  • Light: the standard bright look.
  • Dark: easy on the eyes in a dim room.
  • Business: navy and slate, compact and tidy, like office software.
  • GirlMath: soft pinks, hot-pink accents and rounded corners.
  • Programmer: a green-on-black terminal look with a monospace font and sharp corners.
  • Cozy: autumn creams, browns and burnt orange.
  • Casual: plain greys and one simple blue.
  • ADHD: calm and quiet. Strong colour and bold text are kept for what needs you: amounts owed, budgets that are over, transactions waiting for review and the main button of each page. Everything else is muted, with more space between sections.

Every style keeps text easy to read, and signs, icons and words still say what is good or bad, not only colour. If you use the Light / Dark button at the top right while in another style, it becomes a Style button that opens this picker.

Backups and restore

A backup is a copy of all your data, in case something goes wrong or you move computers. Click Settings, then Backup & data.

  1. Click Back up my data now. You see where it was saved (a folder named backups next to your data; the exact folder is shown at the top of Restore from a backup).
  2. To go back to a backup, scroll to Restore from a backup (not available while you are looking at sample data), find the one you want in the list (Made, Kind and Size) and click Restore….
  3. Type RESTORE and click Restore. The app reloads with your data as it was. Your data from just before is saved first as a "before a restore" backup, so you can undo the restore the same way.

Automatic backups keep the last few; backups you make by hand keep the newest 30.

Export to a spreadsheet

Click Settings, Backup & data, then under Export click Export (name)'s transactions (or Export everyone's transactions if you use a partner). You choose where to save a CSV file with every transaction (date, account, description, category, type, amount) that opens in Excel or any spreadsheet.

Erase your data or delete the app

At the bottom of Backup & data, under Erase and uninstall, you can erase everything or erase everything and remove the app. This cannot be undone.

  1. Click Erase all my data (the app goes back to the welcome screen, as if it were new) or Delete the app and its data (your data is erased first, then the uninstaller starts and the app closes).
  2. Tick Keep one backup copy of my data first (in the backups folder) if you want a last copy. It is the only backup that stays; all others are removed too.
  3. Type the word the app asks for (ERASE or DELETE) and click Erase everything or Erase data and uninstall.

You can also use the installer's wipe option or the Wipe Data tool that comes with the installer.

Common questions

My balance does not match my bank

See Match my bank's balance. In short: import every statement from the start, then use Match my bank's balance on the account's card.

A transaction is in the wrong category

Open Transactions, find it and click its category to choose another. Nothing is deleted; the transaction just moves, and the app remembers it for next time. To fix many at once, see Change many at once.

I imported the same file twice

Do not worry: the app does not record the same transaction twice (see Duplicates). If something did slip in, open Import, find the file under Past imports and click Undo import.

Some transactions are missing from my totals

They are probably waiting. Click Review and answer the questions, and check the Older tab. See Review and Older transactions.

A payment to my loan did not lower the loan

The payment has to be marked as counting toward the loan: see Loans and personal debts. For future payments, tick Always do this when you decide in Review.

Interest or tax is counted wrongly

On Taxes & interest, click the figure, then Remove next to a payment that should not count, or use Add payments… to add one that should. See Taxes and interest.

I deleted or changed something by mistake

  • A wrong category or type: change it again on Transactions.
  • A whole import: Undo import on the Import page.
  • Anything else: restore a backup (see Backups and restore). Your data from just before the restore is saved too.

How do I back up, or move to a new computer?

  1. On the old computer, click Settings, Backup & data, Back up my data now.
  2. Copy the backup file from the backups folder (the place is shown under About) to a USB drive or other safe place.
  3. Install the app on the new computer, start it, and copy the backup file into the backups folder there.
  4. Open Settings, Backup & data, find it under Restore from a backup and click Restore….

How do I start over?

Click Settings, Backup & data, Erase and uninstall and follow Erase your data or delete the app. If you were only trying the app, click Start with my own data instead.

Your privacy

Everything you enter stays on this computer. The app has no account, no cloud, no bank login and no tracking, and it never sends your data anywhere. When you import a file, it is read and then forgotten; the app does not keep a copy of it. Backups are copies of your data on this same computer, so keep them somewhere safe and delete old ones if you give the computer away (Erase your data or delete the app removes them too).
